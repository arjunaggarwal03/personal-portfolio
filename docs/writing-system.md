# Writing system

This site is a working record of judgment. It should make Arjun legible as a
technically serious builder who works close to product and customers, handles
ambiguity, and changes his mind without turning that uncertainty into a brand.

## Voice

The voice is precise, warm, and unfinished.

- **Precise:** Prefer an object, decision, mechanism, number, or consequence to
  a claim about importance. Keep useful technical detail.
- **Warm:** Write in the first person when Arjun is speaking. Let taste, doubt,
  humor, and ordinary interests remain visible.
- **Unfinished:** State the current view without pretending it is final. Name
  what is uncertain and what evidence could change the view.

The prose should sound natural when read to a sharp colleague. Sentence lengths
can vary. A rough edge can stay if it carries voice. Do not make every paragraph
equally polished.

## What each route does

- **Home** gives the current vector: who Arjun is, what he is doing, and where
  to look next.
- **Work** shows judgment under constraints through ownership, mechanisms,
  tradeoffs, and outcomes.
- **Writing** develops finished arguments from experience and open questions.
- **Log** records attention before every observation has to become an argument.
- **Now** is the current edit: active work, a live question, a counterargument,
  evidence that could change the view, and what remains in rotation.
- **About** carries the biography, the uneven path between roles, personal
  interests, and contact details.
- **Early Experiments** preserves evidence of how Arjun learned without
  presenting old projects as current work.
- **Accessibility** is plain operational writing: support, limits, testing, and
  a way to report a problem.

## Claim ownership

Repeat navigation facts when a reader needs them, but give each recurring claim
one main home.

- Current role and a short description of the work: Home.
- Detailed scope, ownership, and results by role: Work.
- Career uncertainty and the personal reasons behind moves: About.
- Arguments about people, models, and workflow state: Writing.
- The question Arjun is actively reconsidering: Now.
- Restaurants, cities, films, music, and saved observations: Log.

## Editing rules

- Protect facts, numbers, mechanisms, dates, and meaningful uncertainty.
- Do not invent chronology, outcomes, opinions, or confidential anecdotes.
- Let evidence carry the emphasis. Cut claims that a detail is important,
  surprising, or insightful when the detail already shows it.
- Cut portable language that could describe any engineer or portfolio.
- Use direct verbs. Prefer “built,” “changed,” and “learned” to abstract phrases
  about impact or growth.
- Avoid resume defensiveness. State the result and its limits once.
- Keep cultural interests free of forced product or engineering lessons.
- Use headings and lists for real structure, not decoration.
- Avoid canned reveals, binary contrasts, summary endings, stacked fragments,
  and decorative em dashes.

## Final check

Before publishing, ask:

1. Is every claim true and supportable?
2. Could this sentence appear unchanged on someone else’s portfolio?
3. Is a detail doing more work than an adjective could?
4. Does uncertainty reflect the facts, or has it become a repeated mannerism?
5. Is this the right route for the claim?
6. Does the ending stop on the last concrete point?

For years, this site was a museum of student projects: a command-line tool, a
hackathon search demo, a parking-pass detector, a screenshot, and a list of
technologies underneath each one.

That was the right site when I was applying for internships. The question it
answered was simple: *Can this person build software?*

It is no longer the question I want the site to answer.

I now care more about the decisions inside the work. What was the real problem?
Which part did I own? What made the obvious approach fail? Who saw the decision
differently? What changed after we shipped it? A stack of project cards leaves
most of that out.

I also don’t want a site that turns my current job into a finished professional
identity. I’m a founding engineer at Lightfield, and much of my attention is on
software used by both people and models. That is the work I happen to be closest
to now. It may become a long-term intellectual direction, or it may lead me
somewhere I cannot see yet. The archive should preserve that uncertainty instead
of editing it out afterward.

So the site now has three main parts.

**Work** explains the trajectory and the systems behind it. I want it to be
clear about ownership, tradeoffs, and outcomes without becoming a second résumé.

**Writing** is where I try to turn experience into an argument. The useful
pieces will probably begin with something I believed too confidently, a system
that behaved differently than expected, or a decision that looked different
depending on where you sat.

**The Log** is less disciplined. It is for restaurants, cities, films, music,
photographs, links, and things I want to remember. Not every interest needs to
become a lesson about software.

The old projects are still here under [Early Experiments](/experiments). Some
were good. Some were mainly excuses to learn a model or system by making it
concrete. Together, they show a path more honestly than a polished selection
would.

If this site works, the reader should not leave with a slogan. They should have
a sense of what I have done, what I notice, how I make decisions, and where my
thinking is still moving.
